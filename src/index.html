IRC client for Windows, Linux, macOS and the browser

Simple IRC Client

Chat on Libera.Chat, OFTC or any other IRC network, from a desktop app or straight from the browser. Private messages between Simple IRC Client users can be end-to-end encrypted.

Open web chat Download

The web chat needs no install or account. Source code is on GitHub, AGPL-3.0.

Encrypted private messages

Direct messages between two Simple IRC Client users can be end-to-end encrypted. The IRC server still relays them; it just can't read them. The same message, from both ends:

The protocol is called SIC-E2EE and is built on the browser's WebCrypto API: a triple Diffie-Hellman handshake over P-256, AES-256-GCM for the messages, and private keys stored as non-extractable keys, so not even the client's own JavaScript can read them. That holds for the web chat too.

Lock icon
Every private conversation shows whether it is encrypted and verified, encrypted but not verified yet, or plain text.
Fingerprints
Read them to each other over a channel you already trust to rule out someone sitting in the middle.
Forward secrecy
Each session uses fresh keys, so a stolen identity key doesn't unlock old conversations.
No silent downgrade
If a peer's key changes, or a conversation drops back to plain text, you get a warning before your next message is sent.
Other clients
People on other IRC clients never see garbage. Their client ignores the unknown CTCP.

Channels aren't encrypted. Everyone in a channel is meant to read it. How the handshake, cipher and key storage work

Stays connected on your phone

Phones cut a browser tab's connection as soon as you switch apps or lock the screen. The web chat's gateway keeps your IRC session open for up to 30 minutes while the tab is away. When you come back you are still in your channels, you see the messages you missed, and nobody saw you quit and rejoin.

Made for everyday chatting

Replies and reactions
Answer a message with its quote above yours, or react with an emoji. On servers without them, a reply goes out as a quoted line.
Long messages
Shift+Enter starts a new line. A message too long for one IRC line is split, and arrives whole where the server allows it.
Spoilers and quotes
Wrap text in || to hide it until clicked, and start a line with > to quote.
History and search
Scroll up for older messages, and search everything in your open windows with Ctrl+Shift+F (⌘⇧F on a Mac).
Less noise
Joins, parts and nick changes fold into one line, and can leave out people who never said anything. Ignore anyone with /ignore.
Never miss a mention
Your nick and words of your choosing are highlighted, with a sound for mentions and private messages.
Keyboard first
Ctrl+K (⌘K on a Mac) switches windows, Tab completes nicks, channels and commands, and Ctrl+/ (⌘/) lists every shortcut.
Your nick back
If your nick is taken, you connect as nick_ and get it back as soon as it is free, or ask NickServ to free it.
Bouncers
Log in to a soju bouncer and pick a network, with the history and read state it keeps for you.
Your way
Light or dark, compact or roomy, 12 or 24-hour time, automatic away after a while, and themes of your own.

Put a chat room on your website

The web client works in an iframe. URL parameters choose the network and the channels, so visitors land straight in your channel.

<iframe
  src="https://app.simpleircclient.com/?server=Libera.Chat&channel=%23your-channel"
  width="100%"
  height="500"
  frameborder="0"
></iframe>
parametervalue
serverNetwork name, such as Libera.Chat or OFTC, or a hostname
portPort number. Defaults to 6697 (TLS)
tlstrue or false
channelChannels to join, comma-separated. Write # as %23
 
# Libera.Chat, #general
https://app.simpleircclient.com/?server=Libera.Chat&channel=%23general

# OFTC, #debian
https://app.simpleircclient.com/?server=OFTC&channel=%23debian

# two channels at once
https://app.simpleircclient.com/?server=Libera.Chat&channel=%23help,%23general

# your own server, over TLS
https://app.simpleircclient.com/?server=irc.example.com&port=6697&tls=true&channel=%23chat
